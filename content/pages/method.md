## Which products

I start from the 50 consumer AI products with the highest observed spending in the United States. Spending is a harder test than downloads or visits: people keep paying only for products they manage to use. The [products page](../products/) gives the source of the ranking and its limits.

## How I use each product

I use each product myself, from the first screen, to get a real piece of work done. I take screenshots as I go and write down what happened at each step, including the steps where I got stuck.

## What a review contains

Every review has two parts.

- **Worth learning from.** What the product's design does well enough that other teams should study it. Each point says what the product does and why it works.
- **Needs improvement.** Where the design gets in the way, and what I would change.

Each point comes with the screenshots it is based on. The review also records the date, the platform, and the plan I used.

## Screenshots

I take every screenshot myself, on the date shown in the review. I crop out personal information. Each one carries a text description for people who cannot see the image. Products change often, so a screenshot shows the product as it was on that date.

## Limits

- These are my notes from my own use. They are not a usability study.
- The ranking counts spending by U.S. consumers. It is not company revenue.
- A design choice appearing in a product people pay for does not prove the choice is the reason they pay.
- A review covers the parts of the product I used. It is not a full audit.

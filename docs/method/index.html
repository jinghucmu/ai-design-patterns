Method

How I choose the products, what I record when I use them, and what a pattern has to contain before it is published.

Which products

I start from the 50 consumer AI products with the highest observed spending in the United States. Spending is a harder test than downloads or visits: people keep paying only for products they manage to use. The products page gives the source of the ranking and its limits.

How I use each product

I use each product myself, from the first screen, to get a real piece of work done. I take screenshots as I go and write down what happened at each step, including the steps where I got stuck.

The four questions

An AI feature is worth something to a person only when they can do four things with it. I record what each product does on each one.

  • Discover

    Can a first-time user find out what the AI can do for them?

  • Direct

    Can they tell it what they want, and adjust when the first result misses?

  • Verify

    Can they check whether the output is right before they rely on it?

  • Recover

    When the AI is wrong, can they correct it or start over without losing their work?

What a pattern contains

A pattern is an interaction that I have seen solve one of these problems. Every pattern on this site has the same five parts:

  • Problem. What the person is trying to do, and what gets in the way.
  • Interaction. What the product does, step by step, with screenshots.
  • Where it fails. The situations in which the pattern stops working or causes harm.
  • Accessibility requirements. What the pattern needs so that people using a screen reader, captions, a keyboard, or larger text can use it too.
  • How to test it. A check a team can run on its own product, without a researcher.

Status

Each pattern carries one of three labels.

  • Observed. Seen in the products listed. Not yet tested with users.
  • Tested. Tested with users. Results are on the page.
  • Withdrawn. Failed in later use. It stays on the site with the reason.

Screenshots

I take every screenshot myself, on the date shown on the product's page. I crop out personal information. Each one carries a text description for people who cannot see the image. Products change often, so a screenshot shows the product as it was on that date.

Limits

  • These are my notes from my own use. They are not a usability study.
  • The ranking counts spending by U.S. consumers. It is not company revenue.
  • A pattern appearing in a product people pay for does not prove the pattern is the reason they pay.
